# Training-program v22 — 2026-11-23

GitHub `main` is the canonical source for the live PWA. The weekly template lives in `program-overrides.json`, gym A/B templates in `gym-overrides.json`, date-based recovery and 14-day exposure tracking in `adaptive.js`.

## Ordered priorities
1. Quad strength and hypertrophy.
2. Biceps hypertrophy, **home only**.
3. Triceps hypertrophy, **home only**.
4. Pull-up repetition skill, no deliberate maximum lat hypertrophy.
5. Neck/shoulder-girdle posture, scapular strength and rotation (high functional priority, not deprioritized).
6. Comprehensive core and pelvic-floor coordination, with **kneeling ab wheel twice weekly**.
7. Hip flexor active strength, especially **floor-seated straight-leg compression over low obstacle**; hip adductor and lateral hip stability, Copenhagen/side plank progression; single-leg glute bridge.
8. Maintain reasonable shoulder strength/handstand skill, dynamic mobility/yoga, hamstring mobility and aerobic fitness.

## Non-negotiables / recurring exposures
- Floor-seated straight-leg lifts on **Tuesday (~2.5 min, 1 set/side) and Friday (~5 min, 2 sets/side)**. Place a low dumbbell or rolled towel ahead of the ankle; lift the *straight* leg over it. Do not chase cramps.
- Active band-resisted standing marches Tuesday; L-sit compression Wednesday/Saturday.
- Hamstring sweeps Monday, Friday and Sunday; can be shortened for recovery but should not disappear from adaptive rebalancing. Dynamic, gentle, no nerve-provocative hamstring stretch.
- Dynamic yoga on Monday and Sunday, shorter selected yoga on Thursday. Favor Cat-Cow, bent-knee Downward Dog / calf pedals, active Low Lunge, pain-free Cobra; Thursday also short Chair/Warrior II/Goddess, thoracic flow, brief 90/90 rotations. No lengthy passive holds or forcing a painful shoulder or front of hip.
- Ab wheel Monday and Thursday; reverse crunch Friday; Copenhagen and side plank Wednesday; glute bridge Wednesday; Back Extension once in Gym A; TRX Face Pull once in Gym B; Chin Tucks and Wall Slides 2–3 times/wk.
- Preserve Turkish Get-Up, DB Windmill, handstand, one low-volume Half-Kneeling DB Shoulder Press, and functional step-ups/thrusters at low-moderate effort if shoulder and knees tolerate.
- Direct arms: biceps Tuesday **4** and Saturday **4+2** = **10** sets/wk; triceps Tuesday **4**, Friday **3 close-grip DB push-ups + 3 lying extensions** = **10** sets/wk. Build to 12 only after observing recovery and progression. Keep appropriate rests and 1–2 RIR on isolations.
- Quads: Gym A **3 split squat + 3 leg extension**; Gym B **2 leg press + 2 split squat + 3 leg extension** = **13** sets over two gym sessions. Do not chase muscle soreness. Gym A also 2 sets Back Extension; Gym B 3 sets TRX Face Pull. Pull-ups remain frequent, not an excessive row/lat volume plan.
- Gym includes equipment-based cardio: 15min easy/moderate bike A; 10min StairMaster B, with easy bike alternative; swimming and brisk walking when possible.

## Adaptive scheduling
- Gym A/B move to the actual day checked by the user. Completed attendance only counts for 14-day training coverage.
- Recovery modifies high-overlap demanding work, but should not generically delete *mandatory* seated floor leg raises, gentle hamstring mobility, or primary home-only arm sessions merely because the gym was checked on the same day.
- Gym A/B coverage entries in the 14-day dashboard represent **daily exposures, not actual logged hard-set counts**. The dashboard should not be described as a complete hard-set tracker.
- Preserved localStorage keys: `workoutSessionLogV2`, `workoutGymLogV1`, `workoutProgressHistoryV1`, `workoutProgressDraftV1`. Do not wipe or migrate without need.

## Recovery/safety
Use 2–3 reps in reserve for heavy compounds, 1–2 for arm isolation, symptom-free ROM, and full relaxation after submaximal pelvic-floor contractions. Respect previous lumbar disc/sciatica, right knee, shoulder and neurological symptoms. If gym falls on an unusually fatiguing day, reduce incidental accessories first and shorten cardio rather than grind through overlapping leg work. Training is not a diagnosis for recurring bilateral foot numbness.
